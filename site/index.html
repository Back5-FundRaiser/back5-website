Most clubs are run by a handful of people with day jobs.

A typical community club has around 150 players and 10 to 20 volunteers. One or two of them carry registrations, messages and payments for everybody else.

We've sponsored clubs, sat on committees and run clubs ourselves. Passion gets a club started. What keeps it going is the off-field side working like a small business: less admin, more ways to earn, and nobody burning out by round six.

How a club gets to self-sustaining

  1. 1

    Put the club on one system

    Rego, payments, messages and schedules move into one app. Automation does the chasing a volunteer used to do.

  2. 2

    Members start helping themselves

    When the app is the only way to register, pay and check the draw, families use it every week without being asked.

  3. 3

    Those habits start to earn

    A community that turns up in one place every week can buy kit, switch phone plans, shop with partners and hear from sponsors there too.

  4. 4

    The club pays its own way

    Lower running costs and several steady incomes add up to a club that makes a surplus and no longer leans on a few people's time and money.

One club network, five ways in.

Start with the one that fixes your biggest headache. Each part works on its own, and they add up when you use more of them.

Club software

Exclusive sports management software, set up and run with you. Rego, payments, messages, scheduling and attendance sit in one app instead of five.

Hours back for your volunteers every week, and one place where every member can be reached.
Club Apparel

Playing and supporter kit made by our apparel partners and ordered through an online club shop.

No more box of spare sizes in someone's garage. The club earns on each order.
Club Mobile

Mobile plans carrying your club's name, offered to members and their families.

A share of every plan comes back to the club each month, for as long as the member stays.
Club Marketplace

Connects your club community to the things they buy every day, through partners who pay the club for the introduction.

Families shop as usual and money lands back in the club's pocket, with no event to run.
